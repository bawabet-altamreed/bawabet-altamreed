🦴 Chapter One

The Bones

🦴 Types of Bones

• Long Bones
• Short Bones
• Irregular Bones
• Flat Bones
• Pneumatic Bones
• Sesamoid Bones

🦴 Long Bones

Elongated bones with two ends and a shaft between them.
The shaft contains bone marrow.

Examples:
• Humerus
• Femur

🦴 Short Bones

Round, cubical or pyramidal bones.

Example:
Carpal Bones.

🦴 Irregular Bones

Short bones without a regular shape.

Example:
Vertebrae.

🦴 Flat Bones

Thin and flat bones.

Examples:
• Skull Bones
• Scapula

🦴 Pneumatic Bones

Bones containing air-filled spaces called Paranasal Sinuses.

They help in voice resonance.

🦴 Sesamoid Bones

Small bones located inside tendons.

Example:
Patella.

🦴 Parts of the Skeleton

The skeleton is divided into two main parts:

1. Axial Skeleton
• Skull
• Vertebral Column
• Ribs
• Sternum

2. Appendicular Skeleton
• Upper Limb Bones
• Lower Limb Bones

💀 The Skull

The skull consists of 21 bones joined together by irregular sutures.

Single Bones:
• Frontal Bone
• Occipital Bone

Paired Bones:
• Parietal Bones
• Temporal Bones

The mandible articulates with the skull in front of the ear.

🧠 Parts of the Skull

• Vault (Superior Part)
• Face (Anterior Part)
• Base (Inferior Part)

The base of the skull contains several foramina through which nerves and blood vessels pass.

⚠️ Fracture of the Skull Base

A fracture of the skull base is dangerous because it may injure blood vessels and cause internal bleeding.

Signs:
• Bleeding from the nose.
• Bleeding from the ears.

👶 Skull of a Newborn Infant

Characteristics:

• No paranasal sinuses.
• Small face.
• Presence of fontanelles.

👶 Fontanelles

Fontanelles are soft membranous areas between the skull bones of a newborn.

There are six fontanelles, but the most important are:

• Anterior Fontanelle
• Posterior Fontanelle

📊 Anterior vs Posterior Fontanelle

Anterior Fontanelle
• Large
• 3 cm
• Diamond-shaped
• Closes after 18–24 months

Posterior Fontanelle
• Small
• 1 cm
• Triangular
• Closes within 3 months

⭐ Importance of Fontanelles

• Determine the child's age.
• Determine fetal position during birth.
• Diagnose rickets.
• Diagnose dehydration.
• Detect increased intracranial pressure.
• Facilitate childbirth.
• May be used for emergency intravenous injection.

💪 Bones of the Upper Limb

The upper limb consists of:

• Clavicle
• Scapula
• Humerus
• Radius
• Ulna
• Hand Bones

🦴 Clavicle

The clavicle is located in the pectoral girdle anteriorly.

It extends between the sternum and the scapula.

🦴 Scapula

The scapula is located in the pectoral girdle posteriorly.

It articulates with:
• Clavicle
• Humerus

🦴 Humerus

The humerus extends between the shoulder and elbow joints.

Superiorly it articulates with the scapula.
Inferiorly it articulates with the radius and ulna.

🦴 Radius

Located on the lateral side of the forearm.

It participates in four joints:
• Elbow Joint
• Wrist Joint
• Superior Radioulnar Joint
• Inferior Radioulnar Joint

🦴 Ulna

Located on the medial side of the forearm.

It participates in:
• Elbow Joint
• Superior Radioulnar Joint
• Inferior Radioulnar Joint

Note: It does not participate in the wrist joint.

✋ Bones of the Hand

The hand consists of three regions:

Carpus
• 8 Carpal Bones

Metacarpus
• 5 Metacarpal Bones

Fingers
• 14 Phalanges
The thumb has only 2 phalanges, while the other fingers have 3 phalanges each.

📋 Summary of Upper Limb Bones

Pectoral Girdle
• Clavicle (Front)
• Scapula (Back)

Arm
• Humerus

Forearm
• Radius (Lateral)
• Ulna (Medial)

Hand
• 8 Carpals
• 5 Metacarpals
• 14 Phalanges

🦵 Bones of the Lower Limb

The lower limb consists of:

• Pelvic Bone
• Femur
• Tibia
• Fibula
• Foot Bones

🦴 Pelvic Bone

The two pelvic bones are connected:

• Anteriorly by the Pubic Symphysis.
• Posteriorly by the Sacrum.

The pelvic bone articulates with the femur at the hip joint.

🦴 Parts of the Pelvic Bone

The pelvic bone consists of three parts:

• Pubis (Anterior)
• Ischium (Posterior)
• Ilium (Superior)

These parts fuse together at puberty.

🦴 Femur

The femur is the longest and strongest bone in the body.

It connects:
• Superiorly with the pelvis.
• Inferiorly with the tibia.

🦴 Tibia

Located on the medial side of the leg.

It bears most of the body's weight.

🦴 Fibula

Located on the lateral side of the leg.

It does not bear the body's weight.

🦶 Bones of the Foot

The foot consists of:

Tarsus
• 7 Tarsal Bones

Metatarsus
• 5 Metatarsal Bones

Toes
• 14 Phalanges

The big toe has 2 phalanges, while the other toes have 3 phalanges each.

⭐ Foot Arches

The foot bones form longitudinal and transverse arches.

These arches protect the nerves and blood vessels of the sole.

Flat foot may cause severe pain during standing.

📋 Summary of Lower Limb Bones

Pelvic Girdle
• Pelvic Bone

Thigh
• Femur

Leg
• Tibia (Medial)
• Fibula (Lateral)

Foot
• 7 Tarsals
• 5 Metatarsals
• 14 Phalanges

🦿 Joints of the Lower Limb

• Hip Joint → Between pelvis and femur.
• Knee Joint → Between femur and leg.
• Ankle Joint → Between leg and foot.

🫁 Thoracic Cage

The thoracic cage consists of:

• Sternum
• 12 Thoracic Vertebrae
• 12 Pairs of Ribs

🦴 Sternum

The sternum is located at the front of the chest.

It consists of:
• Manubrium
• Body
• Xiphoid Process

The second rib articulates at the sternal angle.

🦴 Types of Ribs

True Ribs
Pairs 1–7 (attached directly to the sternum).

False Ribs
Pairs 8–10 (not directly attached).

Floating Ribs
Pairs 11–12 (not attached anteriorly).

🦴 Vertebral Column

The vertebral column consists of 33 vertebrae:

• 7 Cervical
• 12 Thoracic
• 5 Lumbar
• Sacrum
• Coccyx

📍 Parts of a Vertebra

Each vertebra contains:

• Body
• Vertebral Foramen
• Spinous Process
• Transverse Processes

📈 Curves of the Vertebral Column

There are four normal curves:

• Cervical Lordosis
• Thoracic Kyphosis
• Lumbar Lordosis
• Sacral Kyphosis

⭐ Functions of the Vertebral Column

• Protects the spinal cord.
• Bears body weight.
• Forms the central axis of the body.

🦴 The Pelvis

The pelvis consists of:

• Right Pelvic Bone
• Left Pelvic Bone
• Sacrum
• Coccyx

🔗 Pelvic Joints

• Right Sacroiliac Joint
• Left Sacroiliac Joint
• Sacrococcygeal Joint
• Pubic Symphysis

👨👩 Male vs Female Pelvis

Female Pelvis
• Wide inlet
• Wide outlet
• Wide pubic arch (>90°)
• Short, wide sacrum

Male Pelvis
• Heart-shaped inlet
• Narrow outlet
• Narrow pubic arch (<90°)
• Long, narrow sacrum
