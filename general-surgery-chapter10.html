📖 Introduction

  • The mouth is the first part of the digestive system.
  • It receives food and begins digestion.
  • Food is mixed with saliva to facilitate swallowing.
  • The mouth is lined by a delicate mucous membrane.
  • It is surrounded by the maxilla, mandible and tongue.
  • The gums cover the necks of the teeth.
💡 Remember:
The mouth begins digestion by chewing and mixing food with saliva.

🦷 Types of Teeth

  • Primary (Milk) Teeth = 20 teeth.
  • Appear from about the 6th month of age.
  • Later replaced by Permanent Teeth.
  • Permanent Teeth = 32 teeth.
  • Main types:
    • Central Incisor
    • Lateral Incisor
    • Canine
    • Premolars
    • Molars
💡 Milk Teeth = 20
Permanent Teeth = 32

🩺 Diseases of the Mouth & Teeth

Diseases occur mainly in three regions:

  • 🦷 Teeth.
  • 🩹 Mucous membrane, gums and salivary glands.
  • 🦴 Jaw bones.

🦷 Tooth Anatomy

  • The tooth consists of calcified layers surrounding the pulp.
  • The pulp contains nerves and blood vessels.
  • Main layers:
    • Enamel (outer layer).
    • Dentin.
    • Pulp (Tooth Nerve).
💡 Enamel → Dentin → Pulp

🦠 Dental Caries

  • The most common dental disease.
  • Main cause of tooth loss in adults.
  • Occurs due to demineralization of enamel then dentin.
  • Bacteria ferment sugars and produce acids.
  • These acids dissolve calcium and damage the tooth.
⚠️ Dental Caries begins in enamel and progresses toward the pulp if untreated.

⚠️ Causes of Dental Caries

  • Several factors must be present together for caries to occur.
  • 1. Tooth Factors:
    • Deep pits and fissures.
    • Rough tooth surfaces.
    • Crowded teeth retaining food.
  • 2. Bacteria:
    • Especially Streptococcus mutans.
    • Produce acids after fermenting sugars.
  • 3. Fermentable Carbohydrates:
    • Sugars are the main cause.
    • Sticky sweets increase risk.
  • 4. Dental Plaque:
    • Poor oral hygiene.
    • Plaque accumulates bacteria and food debris.
💡 Dental Caries requires: Tooth + Bacteria + Sugar + Plaque.

📋 Types of Dental Caries

  • Pit and Fissure Caries (Most Common).
  • Smooth Surface Caries.
  • Acute Caries.
  • Chronic Caries.
  • Rampant Caries.
💡 Most common type: Pit and Fissure Caries.

🔬 Clinical Manifestation

  • Starts as small white opaque spots.
  • May reach dentin before a visible cavity appears.
  • Caries spreads toward the pulp.
  • Cavity color changes:
    • White.
    • Yellow.
    • Brown.
    • Black.
  • Root caries commonly affects elderly patients.
  • Root caries causes severe sensitivity.

📍 Common Sites of Dental Caries

  • Pits and fissures of molars.
  • Premolars.
  • Buccal surface of molars.
  • Lingual surface of upper incisors.
  • Gingival margin.
  • Rough tooth surfaces.
⚠️ Rough surfaces retain plaque and food, increasing caries risk.

💊 Treatment of Dental Caries

  • Complete removal of decayed tooth tissue.
  • Restore the tooth using suitable filling materials.
  • Amalgam fillings are commonly used for posterior teeth.
  • Composite (Light-cured) fillings are preferred for anterior teeth.

🛡️ Prevention of Dental Caries

  • Brush teeth regularly using fluoride toothpaste.
  • Reduce sugary and sticky foods.
  • Eat foods rich in natural fiber.
  • Increase oral health awareness.
  • Visit the dentist periodically.
  • Replace ill-fitting restorations.
  • Eat fresh fruits to stimulate saliva secretion.
💡 Prevention is always better than treatment.

🦷 Pulpitis

Pulpitis is inflammation of the dental pulp (tooth nerve).

Causes

  • Dental caries.
  • Tooth fracture.
  • Broken filling with recurrent caries.
  • Deep enamel cracks.
  • Blood-borne infection (rare).
  • Chemical irritation.
  • Heat generated during drilling.

Types

  • Acute Pulpitis.
  • Chronic Pulpitis.
  • Chronic Hyperplastic Pulpitis.

🔥 Acute Pulpitis

  • Caused by strong irritants, especially bacteria.
  • Sharp intermittent pain at first.
  • Pain increases with cold drinks.
  • Later becomes continuous.
  • Pain is worse at night.
  • Mild fever may occur.

Treatment

  • Root canal treatment.
  • Removal of inflamed pulp.
  • Extraction if necessary.

🟠 Chronic Pulpitis

  • Caused by weak irritants.
  • Mild tolerable pain.
  • Weak response to hot and cold drinks.
  • Develops over a long period.

Treatment

  • Root canal treatment.
  • Extraction when indicated.

👶 Chronic Hyperplastic Pulpitis

  • Occurs mainly in children.
  • Usually affects primary molars.
  • Large carious cavity is present.
  • Hypertrophied pulp protrudes from the cavity.
  • Pain is usually mild or absent.

Treatment

  • Root canal treatment.
  • Extraction in selected cases.
💡 Hyperplastic pulpitis mainly affects children.

🦷 Attrition, Abrasion & Erosion

Types of Tooth Wear

  • Attrition: Physiological wearing of enamel with aging.
  • Abrasion: Mechanical wearing caused by friction.
  • Erosion: Chemical destruction of enamel by acids.

Causes of Abrasion

  • Using a hard toothbrush.
  • Incorrect horizontal brushing.
  • Bad habits such as holding nails or needles between the teeth.

Causes of Erosion

  • Exposure to acid vapors.
  • Frequent acidic drinks.
  • Gastric acid reflux.

Treatment

  • Dental crowns.
  • Dental bridges when needed.
💡 Attrition = Aging.
Abrasion = Friction.
Erosion = Acid.

🩺 Gum Diseases

The most common gum diseases include:

  • Chronic Periodontitis.
  • Acute Necrotizing Ulcerative Gingivitis (ANUG).
  • Gingival Hyperplasia and Hypertrophy.

🦷 Chronic Periodontitis

Causes

  • Poor oral hygiene.
  • Plaque accumulation.
  • Incorrect tooth brushing.
  • Mouth breathing.
  • Diabetes mellitus.
  • Vitamin C deficiency.

Symptoms

  • Dark red gums.
  • Swollen gums.
  • Bleeding during brushing.
  • Calculus deposits.
  • Periodontal pockets.
  • Gum recession.
  • Loose teeth.
  • Bone destruction in severe cases.

Treatment

  • Remove the cause.
  • Scaling and periodontal care.
  • Good oral hygiene.
  • Antibacterial mouthwash.
⚠️ Untreated chronic periodontitis may lead to tooth loss.

🦠 Acute Necrotizing Ulcerative Gingivitis (ANUG)

Causes

  • Poor oral hygiene.
  • Plaque and calculus accumulation.
  • Stress and reduced immunity.
  • Spirochete bacteria.

Symptoms

  • Mild fever.
  • Painful gingival ulcers.
  • Severe gum bleeding.
  • Bad mouth odor.
  • Enlarged submandibular lymph nodes.
  • Severe cases may spread to jaw bones.

Treatment

  • Remove the cause.
  • Professional gum cleaning.
  • Metronidazole (Flagyl).
  • Good oral hygiene.
  • Hydrogen peroxide mouthwash.
💡 ANUG is strongly associated with Spirochete bacteria.

🦷 Gingival Hyperplasia & Hypertrophy

Causes

  • Hereditary diseases.
  • Long-term use of Dilantin.
  • Some antihypertensive drugs.
  • Hormonal disorders.
  • Chronic gingival inflammation.

Symptoms

  • Increase in gum tissue.
  • Swollen gums.
  • Redness.
  • Sometimes complete coverage of the tooth crown.

Treatment

  • Treat the underlying cause.
  • Periodontal surgery if necessary.
  • Treat associated inflammation.

🦴 Diseases of the Jaw Bones

  • Bacterial infections.
  • Congenital defects.
  • Benign and malignant tumors.
  • Hormonal disorders.

🦠 Dental Abscess

A bacterial infection around the root of the tooth.

Symptoms

  • Severe pain on biting.
  • Red swollen gums.
  • Loose tooth.
  • Fever.
  • Swollen lymph nodes.
  • Pus discharge in chronic cases.
  • Facial swelling.

Treatment

  • Antibiotics.
  • Analgesics.
  • Drainage of pus.
  • Root canal treatment.
  • Extraction if required.
⚠️ Dental abscess requires prompt treatment to prevent spread of infection.

🧬 Congenital Diseases Affecting Teeth

Down Syndrome

  • Delayed eruption of teeth.
  • Small peg-shaped teeth.
  • Large fissured tongue.
  • High incidence of gingivitis.
  • Mouth breathing due to enlarged tonsils.

Ectodermal Dysplasia

  • Missing or few teeth.
  • Poor salivary gland development.
  • Dry mouth.
  • Defective nails, hair and skin.

⏳ Delayed Tooth Eruption

Causes

  • Crowding of teeth.
  • Supernumerary teeth.
  • Cysts.
  • Down Syndrome.
  • Osteomalacia.
  • Radiotherapy.
  • Hypothyroidism.

⚕️ Dental Precautions for Medical Diseases

  • Short dental appointments.
  • Morning appointments are preferred.
  • Careful use of local anesthesia.
  • Check bleeding profile before extraction if needed.
  • Give antibiotics when indicated.
  • Control blood sugar in diabetic patients.
  • Strict sterilization for hepatitis patients.
💡 Always assess the patient's medical history before dental treatment.

🧼 Sterilization & Infection Control

  • Use Steam Autoclave.
  • Sterilize stainless steel instruments.
  • Wear gloves, mask and gown.
  • Follow infection control guidelines.
  • Prevent cross infection between patients.
⚠️ Infection control is essential in every dental clinic.

📌 Chapter Summary

  • Dental caries is the most common dental disease.
  • Streptococcus mutans is the main cause of dental caries.
  • Pulpitis may be acute or chronic.
  • Attrition, abrasion and erosion are types of tooth wear.
  • Periodontitis is the commonest gum disease.
  • ANUG is caused mainly by spirochetes.
  • Dental abscess is treated with drainage and antibiotics.
  • Down Syndrome may delay tooth eruption.
  • Medical history is essential before dental treatment.
  • Steam autoclave is the standard method for sterilizing dental instruments.
