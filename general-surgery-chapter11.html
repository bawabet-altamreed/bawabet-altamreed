🦴 Fractures

Fracture = Separation of a bone into two or more parts.

Types

  • Simple (Closed).
  • Compound (Open).
  • Pathological.
  • Comminuted.

Shapes

  • Transverse.
  • Oblique.
  • Spiral.
  • Impacted.
  • Greenstick.
  • Depressed.

Symptoms

  • Pain.
  • Swelling.
  • Deformity.
  • Loss of movement.
  • Crepitus.
  • Bone may protrude in open fracture.

Diagnosis

  • Clinical Examination.
  • X-Ray.
  • CT Scan (Spinal Fracture).

Complications

  • Shock.
  • Fat Embolism.
  • DVT.
  • Pneumonia.
  • Bed Sores.
  • Infection.
  • Nerve Injury.
  • Gangrene.
  • Non-union & Malunion.
💡 Greenstick fracture occurs in children.

🩹 Splints

Purpose

  • Immobilize fracture.
  • Reduce pain.
  • Protect muscles, nerves & vessels.
  • Prevent skin injury.
  • Prevent bleeding.

Types

  • Rigid Splint.
  • Air Splint.
  • Traction Splint (Thomas).

Rules

  • Immobilize joint above & below fracture.
  • Control bleeding first.
  • Pad pressure points.
  • Do not obstruct circulation.
  • Splint before transport.
⚠️ Every fracture should be splinted before moving the patient.

🦴 Dislocation & Sprain

Dislocation

  • Bone leaves its normal position.
  • Joint deformity.
  • Pain.
  • Loss of movement.
  • Numbness or absent pulse may occur.

Sprain

  • Partial ligament tear.
  • Pain.
  • Swelling.
  • Tenderness.
  • Limited movement.
💡 Dislocation changes joint shape, while sprain usually doesn't.

💪 Upper Limb Injuries

Clavicle Fracture

  • Pain & Swelling.
  • Immobilize with Sling.
  • Figure of 8 Bandage.

Humerus Fracture

  • Pain on movement.
  • Use Sling.
  • Rigid Splint if needed.

Elbow Fracture

  • Severe Pain.
  • Swelling.
  • Internal Bleeding.
  • Immobilize in found position.
  • Never reduce the joint.

Forearm & Wrist Fracture

  • Pain.
  • Deformity.
  • Rigid or Air Splint.
  • Immobilize elbow & wrist.
💡 Immobilize both joints above and below forearm fractures.

🤕 Upper Limb Dislocations

Shoulder Dislocation

  • Anterior or Posterior.
  • Severe Pain.
  • Do NOT reduce.
  • Immobilize with Sling.

Elbow Dislocation

  • Occurs after fall.
  • Posterior Splint.
  • Transport Sitting.

Wrist Dislocation

  • Rare.
  • Apply Splint.
  • Hospital Referral.
⚠️ Never attempt to reduce any dislocated joint during first aid.

🦵 Lower Limb Injuries

Femur Fracture

  • Severe Pain.
  • Short Limb.
  • Foot Rotates Outward.
  • Thomas Splint.
  • Tie Both Legs if needed.

Knee Fracture

  • Pain.
  • Swelling.
  • Apply Splint.
  • Check Circulation.

Tibia & Fibula Fracture

  • Pain.
  • Swelling.
  • Deformity.
  • Rigid, Air or Traction Splint.

Hip / Knee / Ankle Dislocation

  • Immobilize Only.
  • Do NOT Reduce.
  • Transport to Hospital.
💡 Femur fracture is commonly treated with a Thomas traction splint.

🦴 Spine & Skull Injuries

Spinal Fracture

  • May cause spinal cord injury.
  • Pain & tenderness.
  • Back deformity.
  • Paralysis may occur.

First Aid

  • Maintain airway.
  • Immobilize spine.
  • Treat shock.
  • Transport on rigid board.
⚠️ Never move a suspected spinal fracture carelessly.

Skull Fracture

  • Linear.
  • Depressed.
  • Penetrating.

Signs

  • Skull deformity.
  • Blood or CSF from nose/ear.
  • Raccoon eyes.

Care

  • Maintain airway.
  • Control bleeding.
  • Cover wounds.
  • Monitor vital signs.

🫁 Rib Fracture

  • Pain during breathing.
  • Chest tenderness.
  • Risk of Pneumothorax.
  • Elastic chest bandage.
💡 Rib fracture may injure the lung causing pneumothorax.

💿 Disc Herniation

Lumbar Disc

  • Low back pain.
  • Sciatica.
  • Muscle weakness.
  • Rest + Analgesics.
  • Surgery if severe.

Cervical Disc

  • Neck pain.
  • Shoulder pain.
  • Arm pain.
  • Collar + Physiotherapy.

🦿 Amputation

Indications

  • Severe Trauma.
  • Gas Gangrene.
  • Diabetic Gangrene.
  • Malignant Tumors.

🦴 Arthritis

Rheumatoid Arthritis

  • Small joints first.
  • Morning stiffness.
  • Spindle swelling.
  • Ulnar deviation.
  • Cortisone + Physiotherapy.

Osteoarthritis

  • Degenerative disease.
  • Pain on movement.
  • Joint stiffness.
  • Analgesics.
  • Joint Replacement if severe.
💡 Morning stiffness is a classic feature of Rheumatoid Arthritis.

🦠 Osteomyelitis

Acute Osteomyelitis

  • Common in children.
  • Usually caused by Staphylococcus.
  • High fever.
  • Severe bone pain.
  • Swelling.
  • Immobilization + Antibiotics.

Chronic Osteomyelitis

  • Occurs after untreated acute infection.
  • Chronic discharge.
  • Bone thickening.
  • May need surgery.
💡 Acute osteomyelitis is commonly caused by Staphylococcus.

🫁 Tuberculosis of Bones

  • Affects Spine, Hip & Knee.
  • Chronic Pain.
  • Cold Abscess.
  • Muscle Atrophy.
  • Anti-TB Drugs.
  • Surgery if needed.

Pott's Disease

  • Spinal Tuberculosis.
  • Back Deformity.
  • Paraplegia may occur.

🎗 Bone Tumors

Benign

  • Osteoma.
  • Osteochondroma.
  • Chondroma.
  • Bone Cyst.

Malignant

  • Osteosarcoma.
  • Chondrosarcoma.
  • Ewing's Sarcoma.
  • Secondary Tumors.
💡 Osteosarcoma is the most common malignant bone tumor.

👶 Pediatric Bone Diseases

Rickets

  • Vitamin D Deficiency.
  • Calcium Deficiency.
  • Bow Legs.
  • Delayed Walking.

Congenital Hip Dislocation

  • More common in females.
  • Short Limb.
  • Hip Spica Cast.

Clubfoot

  • Congenital Foot Deformity.
  • Casting.
  • Surgery if severe.

Osteoporosis

  • Weak Bones.
  • Common in Elderly.
  • Calcium + Vitamin D.
  • Exercise.

🔬 Arthroscopy

  • Diagnosis & Treatment.
  • Knee & Shoulder.
  • Small Wounds.
  • Fast Recovery.
  • Few Complications.

🦿 Artificial Joint

Aim

  • Pain Relief.
  • Restore Movement.

Indications

  • Osteoarthritis.
  • Rheumatoid Arthritis.
  • Post-fracture Deformity.

Contraindications

  • Septic Arthritis.
  • Paralyzed Muscles.
⚠️ Artificial joints should not be inserted in active septic arthritis.
