Chapter 12

Urinary System

🩺 Components of the Urinary System

  • Upper Urinary Tract: Kidneys + Ureters.
  • Lower Urinary Tract: Urinary Bladder + Urethra.

⚠️ Main Symptoms

  • Renal colic.
  • Kidney pain.
  • Burning during urination.
  • Hematuria (Blood in urine).
  • Lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS).
  • Urinary incontinence.
  • Fever.
  • High blood pressure.
  • Nausea & vomiting in renal failure.

📌 Types of Pain

  • Renal Colic: Usually caused by ureteric stones.
  • Kidney Pain: Usually due to kidney infection.
  • Bladder Pain: Burning during urination with suprapubic pain.

📖 Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms (LUTS)

  • Obstructive: Weak stream, interrupted urination, urinary retention.
  • Irritative: Frequency and urgency of urination.

🚨 Urinary Incontinence

  • Stress Incontinence.
  • Urge Incontinence.
  • Total Incontinence.

🔬 Important Investigations

  • Complete Urine Analysis.
  • Urea & Creatinine.
  • Ultrasound (US).
  • IVP (Contrast X-ray).
  • Renal Scan.
  • CT Scan.
  • Urodynamic Study.
  • Cystoscopy.

🧬 Congenital Defects

  • UPJ Obstruction.
  • Vesicoureteral Reflux (VUR).
  • Urethral Meatus Defects.
  • Urethral Meatal Stenosis.
  • Hypospadias.
  • Epispadias.
  • Congenital Urethral Valves.

🎗️ Urinary Tract Tumors

  • Bladder Tumors.
  • Kidney Tumors.
  • Prostate Tumors.
  • Testicular Tumors.

🩸 Bladder Tumor

  • Most Common Symptom: Hematuria.
  • Best Investigation: Cystoscopy.
  • Superficial: TUR + Intravesical BCG.
  • Invasive: Radical Cystectomy.

🩺 Kidney Tumor

  • Main Cause: Smoking.
  • Symptoms: Hematuria, Flank Mass, Weight Loss.
  • Diagnosis: Ultrasound + CT Scan.
  • Treatment: Radical Nephrectomy.

👨‍⚕️ Prostate Tumors

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

  • Affects men over 50 years.
  • Causes obstructive & irritative urinary symptoms.
  • May lead to urinary retention.
  • Diagnosis: DRE, Ultrasound, PSA, Urodynamics.
  • Treatment: Alpha blockers or TUR-P.

Prostate Cancer

  • Usually affects men over 60 years.
  • High PSA level.
  • Treated by Radical Prostatectomy or Radiotherapy.

🪨 Urinary Tract Stones

  • Most Common: Calcium Oxalate.
  • Uric Acid Stones (Radiolucent).
  • Struvite Stones.
  • Cystine Stones.

Symptoms

  • Renal colic.
  • Hematuria.
  • Recurrent UTI.

Treatment

  • Stone dissolution (Uric acid only).
  • ESWL.
  • Endoscopic removal.
  • Open surgery if needed.

💧 Prevention of Stones

  • Drink 2–3 liters of water daily.
  • Reduce foods rich in uric acid.
  • Treat urinary infections.
  • Analyze the stone composition.

🦠 Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

  • Most Common Route: Ascending infection.
  • Most Common Cause: Gram-negative bacteria.
  • Main Investigation: Urine Culture & Sensitivity.
  • Main Treatment: Appropriate antibiotics.
  • Persistent fever > 3 days → Hospitalization.

🧪 MSU Sample Collection

  1. Clean external genitalia.
  2. Discard first urine.
  3. Collect midstream urine in a sterile container.

🩺 Renal Failure

Types

  • Acute Renal Failure.
  • Chronic Renal Failure.
  • End-stage Renal Failure.

Common Causes

  • Diabetes Mellitus.
  • Hypertension.
  • Chronic UTI.
  • Polycystic Kidney Disease.
  • Urinary Tract Obstruction.

Treatment

  • Treat the cause.
  • Monitor fluid intake & output.
  • Daily weight.
  • Low protein & low potassium diet.
  • Dialysis if needed.
  • DJ Stent or PCN for obstruction.

🚨 Acute Urinary Retention

  • Definition: Inability to urinate with painful full bladder.
  • Main Causes: BPH, urethral stricture, bladder stone.
  • Treatment: Foley catheter.
  • If catheter fails → Suprapubic catheter.

⚠️ Chronic Urinary Retention

  • Residual urine more than 100 ml.
  • May cause Overflow Incontinence.
  • May lead to Obstructive Renal Failure.
  • Treatment: Catheter + Treat the cause.

🏥 Complications of Urinary Surgery

  • Bleeding.
  • Urinary leakage.
  • Wound or urinary infection.
  • Pain.
  • Urinary retention.
  • Electrolyte imbalance.
  • Blocked urinary catheter.

🩸 Urinary System Injuries

Kidney Injury

  • Hematuria.
  • Hematoma or Urinoma.
  • Bed rest and monitoring.
  • Surgery if severe.

Ureter Injury

  • Usually after pelvic surgery.
  • Treatment: DJ Stent or Surgical repair.

Bladder Injury

  • Common with pelvic fractures.
  • Hematuria is common.
  • Treatment: Surgical repair.

⭐ Important Notes

  • Hematuria is the most important symptom of urinary tract tumors.
  • Cystoscopy is the gold standard for bladder tumor diagnosis.
  • Ultrasound is the basic investigation for urinary diseases.
  • Smoking is a major risk factor for kidney and bladder cancer.
  • Gram-negative bacteria are the most common cause of UTI.
  • ESWL is a common non-surgical treatment for urinary stones.
  • Foley catheter is the first treatment for acute urinary retention.
  • Dialysis is indicated in severe renal failure.

📝 Key Points

  • Upper urinary tract = Kidneys + Ureters.
  • Lower urinary tract = Bladder + Urethra.
  • Most common stone = Calcium Oxalate.
  • Radiolucent stone = Uric Acid.
  • Most common UTI route = Ascending infection.
  • Most common bladder tumor symptom = Hematuria.
  • Best bladder investigation = Cystoscopy.
  • Best initial imaging = Ultrasound.
  • BPH usually affects men over 50 years.
  • Prostate cancer commonly affects men over 60 years.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Know the difference between renal colic, kidney pain and bladder pain.
  • Remember the investigations for each urinary disease.
  • Differentiate BPH from Prostate Cancer.
  • Memorize stone types and their treatment.
  • Focus on urinary retention management.
