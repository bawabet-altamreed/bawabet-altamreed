Chapter 13

Anesthesia

📖 Definition of Anesthesia

Temporary loss of sensation in part or all of the body using anesthetic drugs to perform surgery without pain.

🩺 Types of Anesthesia

  • General Anesthesia
  • Regional (Local) Anesthesia

💉 General Anesthesia

  • Loss of consciousness.
  • Loss of pain sensation.
  • Muscle relaxation.

📍 Regional / Local Anesthesia

  • Local infiltration.
  • Nerve block.
  • Plexus block.
  • Spinal anesthesia.
  • Epidural anesthesia.

✅ Pre-anesthetic Assessment

  • Medical history.
  • Physical examination.
  • Laboratory investigations.
  • Patient reassurance.
  • Premedication.
  • Consent.
  • Fasting.
  • Preparation of surgical site.

📋 Important Investigations

  • CBC.
  • Coagulation profile.
  • Liver function.
  • Kidney function.
  • ECG.
  • Chest X-ray.
  • Electrolytes (Na & K).
  • Blood preparation if needed.

💊 Premedication

  • Diazepam (Valium).
  • Midazolam (Dormicum).
  • Flumazenil → Diazepam antidote.
  • Morphine → Pain relief.
  • Continue important drugs (BP, Heart, Steroids).
  • Zantac + Primperan → Reduce gastric acidity.

✍️ Consent & Fasting

  • Written surgical & anesthesia consent.
  • High-risk consent if needed.
  • Fasting at least 6 hours.
  • Infants: 4 hours.
  • Emergency cases may bypass fasting.

🩺 Nurse Before Anesthesia

  • Prepare patient notes.
  • Send required investigations.
  • Confirm fasting.
  • Give medications as ordered.
  • Prepare operating room.
  • Calm the patient.
  • Transfer patient safely.

😴 General Anesthesia Stages

  • Induction.
  • Maintenance.
  • Monitoring.
  • Recovery.

💉 IV Induction Drugs

  • Thiopental.
  • Ketamine.
  • Etomidate.
  • Rapid loss of consciousness.
  • Dose depends on weight.

🌬️ Inhalational Agents

  • Halothane.
  • Isoflurane.
  • Sevoflurane.
  • Nitrous Oxide.
  • Used with Oxygen.

💪 Muscle Relaxants

  • Facilitate endotracheal intubation.
  • Relax abdominal muscles.
  • Require artificial ventilation.
  • Succinylcholine acts within 30 seconds.
  • Duration about 10 minutes.

💉 Narcotic Analgesics

  • Morphine.
  • Fentanyl.
  • Pethidine.
  • Naloxone → Morphine antidote.
  • Used for pain control.

📊 Monitoring During Anesthesia

  • ECG.
  • Heart Rate (HR).
  • Blood Pressure (BP).
  • Temperature.
  • SpO₂.
  • End-tidal CO₂ (EtCO₂).

✅ Recovery Stage

  • Stop anesthetic drugs.
  • Give Prostigmin + Atropine.
  • Remove endotracheal tube.
  • Suction airway.
  • Transfer to recovery room.

🛏️ Spinal Anesthesia

  • Anesthesia of lower half of body.
  • Used for lower limb surgeries.
  • Uses Heavy Marcaine.
  • Can use Fentanyl or Morphine.
  • Requires strict sterility.

⚠️ Spinal Anesthesia Complications

  • Hypotension.
  • Bradycardia.
  • Respiratory difficulty.
  • Headache.
  • Treated according to cause.

🏥 Recovery Room

  • Patient stays until fully recovered.
  • Normal consciousness.
  • Stable breathing.
  • Stable circulation.
  • No active bleeding.
  • Pain under control.

⚠️ Recovery Room Complications

  • Airway obstruction.
  • Hypotension.
  • Hypertension.
  • Vomiting.
  • Convulsions.
  • Bronchospasm.

👩‍⚕️ Nurse Role in Recovery

  • Give oxygen.
  • Monitor ECG & SpO₂.
  • Measure BP every 10 minutes.
  • Observe wound bleeding.
  • Raise bed rails.
  • Report abnormal vital signs.

🚑 ICU Transfer

  • For critical patients.
  • Reserve ICU bed before surgery.
  • Transfer with doctor & nurse.
  • Continue oxygen.
  • Continue monitoring.
  • Maintain patient safety.
