🫀 Shock

Shock is a drop in blood circulation caused by an imbalance between blood volume and circulatory capacity, leading to inadequate oxygen supply to body tissues.

💡 Exam Tip:
Shock = Inadequate blood circulation → ↓ Oxygen supply to tissues.

📋 Types of Shock

  • Surgical Shock
  • Neurogenic Shock
  • Septic Shock

⚠️ Stages of Shock

1️⃣ Reversible Shock

Responds to treatment with improvement in pulse, blood pressure and urine output.

2️⃣ Irreversible Shock

Does not respond to treatment and the patient shows no improvement in vital signs.

💡 Exam Tip:
Reversible = Responds to treatment.
Irreversible = No response to treatment.

🩸 Surgical Shock

Causes

  • Massive blood loss.
  • Fluid loss.
  • Burns.
  • Vomiting.
  • Severe diarrhea.

Symptoms

  • Pale face.
  • Profuse sweating.
  • Rapid weak pulse.
  • Low blood pressure.
  • Rapid shallow breathing.

Treatment

  • Lay the patient flat with the head lowered.
  • Administer blood intravenously.
  • Give IV fluids (Glucose Saline, Ringer's, Lactated Ringer's).
  • Administer vasopressors and Hydrocortisone.
  • Treat the underlying cause.

🧠 Neurogenic Shock

Cause

  • Severe pain.

Symptoms

  • Slow weak pulse.
  • Low blood pressure.
  • Pallor.
  • Profuse sweating.

Treatment

  • Lay the patient flat with the head lowered.
  • Stimulate the patient.
  • Administer IV fluids.
💡 Exam Tip:
Neurogenic Shock is commonly caused by severe pain.

🦠 Septic Shock

Cause

Severe infection such as pneumonia, urinary infection, gangrene, internal abscess or advanced peritonitis.

Symptoms

  • High temperature.
  • Rapid pulse.
  • Low blood pressure.
  • Local signs of infection.

Treatment

  • Treat the cause (usually surgery).
  • Give IV fluids, plasma and blood.
  • Administer strong antibiotics.
  • Give IV Zantac to prevent gastric ulcers.
  • Improve breathing to increase oxygen.
⚠️ Important:
Treating the infection early is essential to prevent septic shock complications.
