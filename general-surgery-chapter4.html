📚 Postoperative Complications

Surgical complications may result from the patient's original disease, the disease requiring surgery, the operation itself, or anesthesia.

Types of Surgical Complications

  • Complications of Anesthesia.
  • Complications of Fluid Transfusion.
  • Complications of the Wound.
  • Respiratory Complications.
  • Cardiac & Venous Complications.
  • Digestive System Complications.
  • Liver Complications.
  • Kidney & Urinary Complications.
  • Postoperative Fever.
💡 Exam Tip:
Always remember the complications are divided into nine major groups.

💉 Complications of Anesthesia

General Anesthesia

  • Malignant Hyperthermia.
  • Nausea & Vomiting.
  • Vocal Cord Spasm.
  • Urinary Retention.
  • Hypothermia.
  • Nerve Injury (Rare).

Malignant Hyperthermia

May occur during surgery or within 24 hours after surgery.

Symptoms
  • High Temperature.
  • Increased Pulse.
  • High Blood Pressure.
  • Muscle Rigidity.

Spinal Anesthesia

  • Hypotension.
  • Respiratory Disturbance.
  • Headache.
  • Urinary Retention.
  • Meningitis (Very Rare).
  • Nerve Injury (Very Rare).
💡 Exam Tip:
Malignant Hyperthermia is the most famous complication of General Anesthesia.

💧 Complications of Fluid Transfusion

Local Complications

1️⃣ Leakage Around Cannula

  • Swelling.
  • Pain.
  • Stopping of IV Drip.
Treatment
  • Move Cannula.
  • Apply Ointment.

2️⃣ Phlebitis

  • Swelling.
  • Bluish Discoloration.
  • Pain.
  • Warm Vein.
Treatment
  • Stop IV.
  • Warm Compresses.
  • Anti-inflammatory Ointment.

3️⃣ Cannula Infection

Prevent by good sterilization before cannulation.

⚠️ Important:
Always use aseptic technique while inserting the cannula.

💧 General Complications of Fluid Transfusion

1️⃣ Pyrogenic Reaction

A sudden rise in temperature during or after IV fluid administration due to contaminated solutions.

Symptoms

  • High Temperature.
  • Chills.
  • Shivering.

Treatment

  • Stop the IV infusion.
  • Give Anti-allergy drugs.
  • Apil or Decadron.

2️⃣ Air Embolism

Rare complication during insertion of Central Venous Catheter.

Prevention

  • Prevent air entering the catheter.
  • Clamp catheter during changing fluids.
  • Use Three-way stopcock.

Symptoms

  • Shock.
  • Cyanosis.

Treatment

  • Lower head of bed.
  • Patient lies on left side.
  • Give Oxygen.

3️⃣ Pulmonary Embolism

Occurs when a clot travels to pulmonary vessels.

Symptoms

  • Rapid Pulse.
  • Rapid Respiration.
  • High Blood Pressure.
  • Cyanosis.

Treatment

  • Transfer to ICU.
  • Give Oxygen.
  • Artificial Ventilation if needed.
  • Heparin.
  • Streptokinase.

4️⃣ Fluid Overload

Occurs after rapid administration of large amounts of IV fluids.

Symptoms

  • Dyspnea.
  • Cyanosis.
  • Neck Vein Distension.
  • Hypertension.
  • Cough.
  • Edema.

Treatment

  • Stop IV Fluids.
  • Sitting Position (45°).
  • Lasix.
💡 Exam Tip:
Air Embolism → Left Side + Oxygen.

🩹 Wound Complications

1️⃣ Hematoma

  • Bleeding after surgery.
  • Due to surgical error or bleeding tendency.

2️⃣ Seroma

Collection of serous fluid under the skin.

Treatment

  • Pressure Dressing.
  • Alpha Chymotrypsin.
  • Aspiration if large.

3️⃣ Wound Dehiscence

Occurs due to improper muscle suturing.

Signs

  • Serous discharge.
  • Wound opens.

Treatment

  • Urgent Exploration.
  • Resuturing.
⚠️ Important:
Serous discharge before wound opening is an important warning sign.

🫁 Respiratory Complications

1️⃣ Atelectasis

Caused by bronchial obstruction and weak breathing.

Symptoms

  • Fever.
  • Rapid Pulse.
  • Rapid Respiration.

Prevention

  • Early Ambulation.
  • Breathing Exercises.

Treatment

  • Bronchodilators.
  • Expectorants.

2️⃣ Bronchitis

Occurs after aspiration of gastric contents.

Symptoms

  • High Temperature.
  • Dyspnea.
  • Low Oxygen.

Treatment

  • Tracheal Suction.
  • Hydrocortisone.
  • Antibiotics.

3️⃣ Pneumonia

Infection of the lung by microorganisms.

Diagnosis

  • X-ray.
  • CT Scan.

Treatment

  • Antibiotics.
  • Rest.
  • Antipyretics.
  • Expectorants.
  • Ventilator if severe.
💡 Exam Tip:
Early ambulation after surgery helps prevent Atelectasis.

Local Complications

  • Leakage around Cannula.
  • Phlebitis.
  • Cannula Site Infection.

General Complications

  • Pyrogenic Reaction.
  • Air Embolism.
  • Pulmonary Embolism.
  • Fluid Overload.
⚠️ Important:
Air embolism → Left lateral position + Oxygen.

🩹 Wound Complications

  • Hematoma.
  • Seroma.
  • Wound Dehiscence.
💡 Exam Tip:
Seroma = Collection of serous fluid.
Hematoma = Collection of blood.

🫁 Respiratory Complications

  • Atelectasis.
  • Bronchitis.
  • Pneumonia.
⚠️ Early ambulation after surgery helps prevent Atelectasis.

❤️ Cardiac & Venous Complications

  • Cardiac Arrhythmias.
  • Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT).
  • Pulmonary Embolism (PE).

Prevention of DVT

  • LMWH (Clexane).
  • Avoid dehydration.
  • Early mobilization.
💡 Exam Tip:
DVT → Swollen painful leg.
PE → Sudden dyspnea + Cyanosis.

🍽️ Digestive Complications

  • Paralytic Ileus.
  • Gastric Dilatation.
  • Intestinal Obstruction.
  • Fecal Impaction.
⚠️ Gastric Dilatation is treated with an NG tube and suction.

🟤 Liver Complications

  • Hepatitis.
  • Viral Hepatitis.
  • Obstructive Jaundice.

🟡 Kidney & Urinary Complications

  • Urinary Retention.
  • Urethritis.
💡 Exam Tip:
Urinary Retention → Catheter if conservative treatment fails.

🌡️ Fever After Surgery

Within 48 Hours

  • Anesthesia.
  • IV Fluids.
  • Blood Transfusion.
  • Atelectasis.

After 48 Hours

  • Wound Infection.
  • Pneumonia.
  • UTI.

Day 5–7

  • Abscess.
  • DVT.

🩸 Blood Transfusion

Complications

  • Fluid Complications.
  • Incompatibility Reaction.
  • Disease Transmission.
  • Jaundice.

Symptoms of Incompatibility

  • Fever & Chills.
  • Rapid Pulse.
  • Hypotension.
  • Dyspnea.
  • Cyanosis.

Treatment

  • Stop Blood Immediately.
  • IV Corticosteroids.
  • Mannitol.
  • Slow IV Calcium.
⚠️ Incompatibility Reaction is the MOST dangerous complication of blood transfusion.

🧬 Blood Groups

  • No Agglutination → O
  • Anti-A only → A
  • Anti-B only → B
  • Both Anti-A & Anti-B → AB

Rh Factor

  • Rh +ve = 85%
  • Rh -ve = 15%
💡 Exam Tip:
Remember:
O → No agglutination.
AB → Both Anti-A & Anti-B.
