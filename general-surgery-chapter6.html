🚑 Injuries

Injuries are a major cause of death. Early diagnosis and correct treatment improve patient survival.

💡 Exam Tip:
Early treatment saves lives.

⏰ Causes of Death after Injuries

1️⃣ Immediate Death (50%)

  • Brain injury
  • Spinal cord injury
  • Heart injury

2️⃣ Early Death (30%)

  • Airway obstruction
  • Respiratory injury
  • Shock

3️⃣ Late Death (20%)

  • Infection
  • Organ failure
💡 Exam Tip:
Immediate = 50%
Early = 30%
Late = 20%

🩺 Patient Assessment

Primary Assessment

Rapid assessment to detect life-threatening conditions.

Secondary Assessment

Complete examination of the patient after stabilization.

🚨 Primary Assessment (ABCDE)

  • A → Airway
  • B → Breathing
  • C → Circulation
  • D → Disability
  • E → Exposure

Main Steps

  • Open airway.
  • Check breathing.
  • Check pulse.
  • Observe injuries.
  • Immobilize suspected neck fracture.
  • Keep patient warm.
💡 Exam Tip:
ABCDE is the first assessment of every trauma patient.

📋 Secondary Assessment

A complete examination after the patient becomes stable.

  • Examine the head and scalp.
  • Check pupils.
  • Examine ears, nose and mouth.
  • Examine neck.
  • Examine chest.
  • Examine abdomen and pelvis.
  • Examine spine.
  • Perform X-ray, CT and Ultrasound when needed.

🚑 Triage

🔴 High Priority

  • Difficulty breathing
  • Severe shock
  • Acute bleeding
  • Open chest or abdominal wounds
  • Cardiac arrest
  • Severe head injuries

🟡 Medium Priority

  • Burns
  • Multiple fractures
  • Spinal fractures

🟢 Low Priority

  • Minor injuries
  • Minor burns
  • Dead patient
💡 Exam Tip:
Difficulty Breathing = Highest Priority.

🧠 Head Injuries

Types

  • Laceration
  • Scalp Contusion
  • Skull Fracture
  • Basilar Skull Fracture

Brain Injury

  • Concussion
  • Post-Concussion
💡 Exam Tip:
Every concussion patient should be observed for 24 hours.

🧮 Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)

GCS evaluates:

  • Eye Opening
  • Motor Response
  • Verbal Response
⚠️ Important:
GCS ≤ 8 requires Endotracheal Intubation and Mechanical Ventilation.

🩸 Brain Hemorrhage

Epidural Hematoma

  • Middle meningeal artery injury.
  • Lucid interval.
  • Needs urgent surgery.

Subdural Hematoma

  • Common in elderly.
  • May be acute or chronic.
  • Treated conservatively or surgically.

Intracerebral Hemorrhage

  • Diagnosed by CT Scan.
  • Treatment depends on severity.

🦴 Neck Injuries

  • Immobilize the neck if fracture is suspected.
  • Do NOT move the patient.
  • X-ray or CT Scan if needed.
  • Doppler if vascular injury is suspected.
💡 Exam Tip:
Suspected Neck Fracture = Cervical Collar + No Movement.

🫁 Chest Injuries

Rib Fracture

  • Pain during breathing.
  • Treated with analgesics.
  • Do NOT strap the chest.

Pneumothorax

  • Air in pleural cavity.
  • Lung collapse.
  • Needs Chest Tube.

Hemothorax

  • Blood in pleural cavity.
  • Also treated with Chest Tube.
⚠️ Tension Pneumothorax is Life Threatening.

🩺 Chest Tube

Indications

  • Pneumothorax
  • Hemothorax
  • Empyema
  • After Chest Surgery

Removed When

  • No Air Leak.
  • No Drainage.
  • Lung Fully Expanded.

🩻 Abdominal Injuries

  • Ultrasound for blunt trauma.
  • CT if patient is stable.
  • Penetrating wounds → Surgery.
  • Cover protruding intestine with warm saline gauze.
  • Never push intestines back.

Spleen Injury

  • Left ribs 9–10.
  • May require Splenectomy.

Liver Injury

  • Right ribs 7–10.

Bowel Injury

  • Air under diaphragm.
  • Penetrating wound.

🦴 Pelvic Injuries

  • Pelvic fracture may cause severe bleeding.
  • Always perform Pelvic X-ray.
  • Check urethral injury before Foley catheter.
💡 Exam Tip:
Blood from urethra = Suspected Urethral Injury.
