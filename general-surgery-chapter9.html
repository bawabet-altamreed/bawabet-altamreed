👂 Ear Anatomy

  • Ear = Organ of Hearing.
  • Divided into 3 Parts:
    • External Ear.
    • Middle Ear.
    • Inner Ear.
  • External Ear:
    • Pinna (Auricle).
    • External Auditory Canal.
    • Tympanic Membrane (Eardrum).
  • Middle Ear:
    • Air-filled cavity.
    • Contains 3 Ossicles:
      • Malleus.
      • Incus.
      • Stapes.
    • Eustachian Tube equalizes pressure.
  • Inner Ear:
    • Cochlea.
    • Auditory Nerve.
    • Hair Cells.
💡 Remember:
External → Middle → Inner Ear.

🔊 Sense of Hearing

  1. Sound waves collected by the Pinna.
  2. Travel through External Auditory Canal.
  3. Hit Tympanic Membrane.
  4. Ossicles amplify vibration.
  5. Stapes transfers vibration to Cochlea.
  6. Hair Cells convert vibration into nerve impulses.
  7. Auditory Nerve carries impulses to Brain.
  8. Brain interprets sound.
💡 Sequence: Pinna → Canal → Tympanic Membrane → Ossicles → Cochlea → Auditory Nerve → Brain.

🟡 Ear Wax (Cerumen)

  • Secreted by Ceruminous Glands.
  • Protects ear by trapping dust.
  • Normally leaves ear naturally.
  • Excess wax may cause:
    • Blocked Ear.
    • Temporary Hearing Loss.
  • Removal methods:
    • Syringing.
    • Suction.
    • Special Instrument.
    • Wax-softening drops before irrigation.
⚠️ Never clean the ear with sharp objects.

🦠 Otitis Externa

Types

1- Diffuse Otitis Externa

  • Pain on pressing the tragus.
  • Narrow External Auditory Canal.
  • Purulent discharge may be present.
  • Treatment:
    • Antibiotics.
    • Ear wick.
    • Topical antibiotic + Anti-inflammatory cream.

2- Furuncle

  • Severe ear pain.
  • Pain increases with chewing and movement of pinna.
  • Narrow auditory canal.
  • Treatment:
    • Antibiotics.
    • Ear wick.
    • Glycerin Ichthyol.

3- Malignant Otitis Externa

  • Usually affects diabetic patients.
  • Severe infection causing bone necrosis.
  • Treatment = Ciprofloxacin (Cipro).

4- Otomycosis

  • Fungal infection.
  • Ear itching.
  • Temporary hearing loss.
  • Ear discharge.
  • Treatment:
    • Repeated cleaning.
    • Topical antifungal drugs.
💡 Malignant Otitis Externa is commonly seen in Diabetic patients.

👂 Otitis Media

Middle ear infection is common in children, especially preschool age.

Causes

  • Upper respiratory infection.
  • Common cold.
  • Sore throat.
  • Allergy.

Routes of Infection

  • Eustachian Tube (Most common).
  • Blood stream.
  • External canal through perforated eardrum.

Types

  • Acute: Ear pain + Fever + Headache + Hearing loss.
  • Chronic: Persistent discharge + Hearing loss + Destruction of ossicles.

Symptoms

Adults

  • Earache.
  • Hearing impairment.
  • Pus discharge.

Children

  • High fever.
  • Crying continuously.
  • Pulling the ear.
  • Purulent discharge.

Diagnosis

  • Otoscope examination.
  • Bulging red Tympanic Membrane.
  • May be perforated.

Complications

  • Damage to Tympanic Membrane.
  • Damage to Ossicles.
  • Auditory Nerve damage.
  • Permanent Hearing Loss.
  • Speech delay in children.

Prevention

  • Do not feed infant lying flat.
  • Do not insert sharp objects into the ear.

Treatment

  • Oral antibiotics.
  • Ear drops.
  • Analgesics.
  • Nasal drops.
  • Clean ear discharge.
  • Myringotomy if pus accumulates.
⚠️ Delayed treatment may cause permanent hearing loss.

🪲 Foreign Bodies in Ear

Types

  • Hard objects (Beads, Pebbles).
  • Plant seeds.
  • Insects.

Treatment

  • Hard objects → Ear irrigation.
  • Plant seeds → Irrigation or Micro-instruments.
  • Insects → Kill with Paraffin Oil then irrigate.
⚠️ Never use forceps for hard foreign bodies.

😴 Snoring

Causes

  • Deviated Nasal Septum.
  • Nasal Allergy.
  • Nasal Polyps.
  • Large Tonsils.
  • Large Uvula.
  • Large Tongue.
  • Obesity.
  • Sedatives.

Complications

  • Poor sleep.
  • Daytime drowsiness.
  • Lack of concentration.
  • Heart & Respiratory problems.

Treatment

  • Weight loss.
  • Treat airway obstruction.
  • Septal surgery.
  • Polyp removal.
  • Tonsillectomy.
  • Uvuloplasty.
  • CPAP device if muscle relaxation is the cause.
💡 The most common reversible cause of snoring is Obesity.

🩸 Epistaxis (Nosebleed)

Epistaxis is a common condition, especially during summer due to dilation of nasal blood vessels.

Causes

General Causes

  • Hypertension.
  • Heart & Blood Vessel Diseases.
  • Liver Diseases.
  • Bleeding Disorders.
  • High Fever (Children).

Local Causes

  • Dilated vessels in anterior nasal septum.
  • Deviated Nasal Septum.
  • Nasal Trauma.
  • Atrophic Rhinitis.
  • Sinusitis.
  • Nasal Tumors.

Questions to Ask

  • Does the patient have hypertension?
  • Any nasal trauma?
  • Taking Aspirin or Anticoagulants?
  • Signs of shock?
  • Is bleeding severe or recurrent?

First Aid

  • Sit leaning forward.
  • Pinch the nose for 5–10 minutes.
  • Breathe through the mouth.
  • Apply ice around the nose.
  • Do NOT tilt the head backward.
  • If bleeding continues → ENT examination.

Treatment

  • Cauterization.
  • Anterior Nasal Packing.
  • Posterior Nasal Packing.
  • Blood transfusion if shock occurs.
  • Surgical ligation in severe cases.
💡 Most epistaxis occurs from the Anterior Nasal Septum.
⚠️ Never tilt the patient's head backward.

🤧 Sinusitis

Inflammation of the Paranasal Sinuses due to blockage of sinus openings.

Paranasal Sinuses

  • Frontal.
  • Ethmoid.
  • Maxillary.
  • Sphenoid.

Acute Sinusitis

Cause

  • Obstruction of sinus openings.

Symptoms

  • Headache.
  • Facial pain.
  • Pain increases on bending forward.
  • Fever.
  • Purulent nasal discharge.
  • Sore throat.

Treatment

  • Antibiotics.
  • Saline nasal wash.
  • Nasal sprays or drops.
  • Analgesics.

Chronic Sinusitis

  • Repeated attacks.
  • Persistent symptoms.
  • Requires longer treatment.
  • May need Endoscopic Sinus Surgery.
💡 Pain increases when bending forward or during prayer.
⚠️ Chronic sinusitis may require surgical intervention.

🦠 Tonsillitis

The tonsils are lymphoid tissues located in the oropharynx and help protect the body from infection.

Types

  • Acute Tonsillitis.
  • Chronic (Recurrent) Tonsillitis.

🔥 Acute Tonsillitis

Causes

  • Viral infection.
  • Bacterial infection (β-Hemolytic Streptococcus is the most common).

Symptoms

  • Severe sore throat.
  • Difficulty swallowing.
  • Fever (up to 40°C).
  • Headache.
  • Loss of appetite.
  • Hot potato voice.
  • Nausea & vomiting (children).

Signs

  • Enlarged tonsils.
  • Red tonsils with white pus spots.
  • Enlarged tender cervical lymph nodes.

Complications

  • Peritonsillar abscess.
  • Dehydration.
  • Rheumatic fever.
  • Acute glomerulonephritis.

Treatment

  • Antibiotics for 7–10 days.
  • Antipyretics.
  • Antiseptic gargles / Lozenges.
  • Plenty of fluids.
  • Rest.
💡 Complete the full antibiotic course even if symptoms improve.

♻️ Chronic Tonsillitis

Repeated attacks (5–6 attacks/year or 3 attacks/year for 2 consecutive years).

Symptoms

  • Recurrent fever.
  • Pain on swallowing.
  • Dry throat.
  • Bad breath.

Signs

  • Large tonsils.
  • Persistent redness.
  • Unequal tonsil size.
  • Enlarged cervical lymph nodes.
  • Snoring or Sleep Apnea.

Treatment

  • Tonsillectomy when indicated.

🏥 Tonsillectomy

Indications

  • Chronic Tonsillitis.
  • Peritonsillar Abscess.
  • Tonsillar Hypertrophy.
  • Sleep Apnea.
  • Suspected Tonsil Tumor.

Contraindications

Absolute

  • Bleeding disorders (Hemophilia).
  • Advanced Heart Disease.
  • Advanced Kidney Disease.
  • Advanced Liver Disease.

Relative

  • Acute Tonsillitis.
  • High fever.
  • Menstruation.
  • Pregnancy.
  • Epidemics (e.g. Polio).

Pre-operative Preparation

  • CBC + Hb.
  • Bleeding Time.
  • Clotting Time.
  • Prothrombin Time.
  • Urine analysis.
  • Heart & Chest examination.
  • Record vital signs.
  • Light dinner.
  • NPO for 6 hours.

Post-operative Care

  • Recovery position (side lying).
  • Observe for bleeding.
  • Cold fluids after recovery.
  • Soft diet.
  • Avoid hot, dry and spicy food for one week.

Complications

  • Bleeding.
  • Anesthesia complications.
  • Wound infection.
⚠️ Post-operative bleeding is the most important complication.

👶 Adenoids

Adenoids are lymphoid tissues located in the nasopharynx behind the nose.

Functions

  • Help fight infection.
  • Support the immune system.

Symptoms of Enlarged Adenoids

  • Mouth breathing.
  • Nasal obstruction.
  • Snoring.
  • Sleep apnea.
  • Recurrent ear infections.
  • Persistent nasal discharge.
  • Poor concentration.

Complications

  • Facial deformity (Adenoid Face).
  • Speech problems.
  • Repeated Otitis Media.
  • Sleep disturbance.

Treatment

  • Adenoidectomy.
💡 Enlarged adenoids are the most common cause of snoring in children.

🫁 Laryngeal Obstruction

Causes

  • Laryngeal edema.
  • Allergic reactions.
  • Smoke or chemical inhalation.
  • Laryngitis.
  • Vocal cord paralysis.
  • Laryngeal tumors.
  • Foreign body.
  • Trauma.

Symptoms

  • Difficulty breathing.
  • Stridor.
  • Hoarseness.
  • Weakness.
  • Profuse sweating.

Signs

  • Rapid pulse.
  • Rapid respiration.
  • Chest retraction.
  • Nasal flaring.
  • Cyanosis (late sign).
  • Low blood pressure.

Treatment

  • Oxygen.
  • Corticosteroids.
  • Decongestants.
  • Emergency Tracheostomy if severe.
⚠️ Severe laryngeal obstruction is a medical emergency.

🩺 Tracheostomy

Opening the trachea and inserting a tube to maintain the airway.

Indications

  • Laryngeal obstruction.
  • Retention of secretions.
  • Coma patients.
  • Preparation for some surgeries.

Post-operative Care

  • Semi-sitting position.
  • Regular suction of secretions.
  • Clean inner tube.
  • Monitor bleeding.
  • Monitor fever.
  • Monitor breathing.
  • Humidify inspired air.
  • Patient speaks by temporarily closing the tube after recovery.
💡 Semi-sitting position helps drainage of secretions and improves breathing.
